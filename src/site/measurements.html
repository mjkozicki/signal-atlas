READ THE NUMBERS

Read the numbers with context

Every value describes the selected observation and the sensor that collected it.

RSSI & dBm

RSSI means Received Signal Strength Indicator. Here, the signal value is reported in dBm: power relative to one milliwatt on a logarithmic scale. A less negative value is stronger: −50 dBm is stronger than −70 dBm.

ILLUSTRATION · NOT A LIVE READING
−90 · weaker−30 · stronger

1× the received power of −60 dBm. A 10 dB increase means ten times the power; it does not mean ten times the speed.

Walls, orientation, interference, distance, and adapter calibration affect readings. Signal alone cannot establish throughput, reliability, or distance. NFC does not report RSSI here. Radio measurement reference ↗

Band, channel & channel width

Band · GHz
The frequency range, such as 2.4, 5, or 6 GHz. GHz means billions of cycles per second; it is not a data rate.
Frequency · MHz
A radio frequency in millions of cycles per second. For example, 2412 MHz is a frequency in the 2.4 GHz band.
Channel
A numbered frequency assignment within a band. Read the band together with the channel; channel numbers can repeat across bands.
Width · MHz
The amount of spectrum a channel uses, such as 20, 40, or 80 MHz. Wider channels occupy more spectrum; they do not guarantee faster service. This parser may report unknown or only a basic width hint.
Wi-Fi generation
A capability hint from decoded announcements. It is not the speed negotiated by a client; advanced generations and features may be unrecognized.

Counts, timestamps & RF charts

First / last seen bound the observations within a snapshot, not the lifetime of a device. Wi-Fi uses capture timestamps; Bluetooth/NFC use the local collection clock. The dashboard displays times in your browser’s local timezone.

  • Wi-Fi observations: accepted beacon/probe-response observations grouped by BSSID.
  • Bluetooth observations: provider advertisement callbacks, not a raw RF packet count.
  • NFC observations: tag presentations; leaving a tag on the reader does not repeatedly increment the count.

The Wi-Fi curves show received signal by AP, ordered by name. Primary channel occupancy counts APs, not busy-airtime percentage. Counts alone cannot identify interference or a best channel.

The app does not measure internet speed, latency, packet loss, noise floor, or signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). An unknown value or “—” means unavailable, not zero.

Configuration score · 0–100

Wi-Fi scoring covers only inventory-matched APs. Each starts at 100 and loses points for its findings, with a minimum of zero.

−40Critical−25High−10Medium−3Low

Example: one high and one medium finding gives an AP 65 / 100.

The displayed score is the mean of authorized AP scores, rounded down. With no authorized APs, or incomplete security decoding on an authorized AP, the score is unknown.

A high score means fewer findings under the current rules. It does not certify password strength, firmware, client behavior, or overall security. Bluetooth and NFC have no security score.
